Counter

Privacy

Counter keeps a small business's records on the phone that runs it. Nothing is uploaded unless you switch something on, and there are exactly two things that can send data off the device. Both are listed below, including the one that sends a recording without removing names from it.

What Counter records

Whatever you enter: customers and their phone numbers, visits, payments, money owed, prices, reminders, renewal dates, and any notes you write. Counter also derives figures from those records — who has gone quiet, what is overdue, how a month compares — but those are calculated on the phone each time and not stored separately.

Records on the phone are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, using a key generated on the device and held in its secure hardware. That key never leaves the phone, which also means nobody else can decrypt the phone's copy — including us.

You do not need an account

The app is complete without one. There is no sign-up to use it, no email address, and no password. A phone number is only involved if you turn on cloud backup, which is described below and is off until you do.

The three optional things

Each is off by default. Turning one on is the only way data leaves the device.

1 · Cloud backup

If you enable it, your records are copied to our server so a lost phone does not mean a lost business. Signing in uses your phone number and a one-time code — no email, no social account. The sign-in is handled by Clerk, and the records are stored with Supabase.

Be clear about what this changes: your cloud backup is not end-to-end encrypted. Customer names, phone numbers and notes are stored as ordinary database fields, protected by access rules that tie every row to your account. That is a genuine protection against other users, and it is not a protection against us — we could read a backup if we chose to. The encryption described above covers the phone's own copy, not the server's. If that matters to you, leave cloud backup off; everything else in the app still works.

2 · The assistant

Most questions are answered by the phone itself, offline, with nothing sent anywhere. When a question is one the device cannot work out, it can be passed to a language model at Sarvam AI, an Indian provider — but only if you have connected a model key of your own.

Before a typed question is sent, names, phone numbers and free text are replaced with tokens — a customer becomes P4 — and put back after the answer returns. The model is never given a name, and it is never asked to produce a figure: it chooses which of seven lookups to run, and the phone does the arithmetic.

3 · Importing contacts

You can import customers from your phone's contacts instead of typing them. Counter reads the names and numbers you select and saves them as ordinary records. It does not upload your address book. Those records then behave like any other — which means they are included in a cloud backup if you have one.

Spoken questions are the exception

If you ask a question out loud, the recording is sent to Sarvam AI exactly as it was made, including any name you said. This is the one place the app cannot protect you the way it protects typed questions, and the reason is unavoidable: audio cannot have names removed before it is transcribed, because the transcription is the thing that would reveal a name was in it.

The exposure stops there. What comes back is put through the same tokenising as a typed question before it goes any further, the recording is not kept after transcription, and the screen with the microphone on it says this in plain words rather than burying it here. If you would rather not, type instead — every question works either way.

What Counter never collects

  • No analytics, and no usage tracking of any kind.
  • No advertising identifiers, and no advertising.
  • No location. The app blocks the location permissions outright, so it cannot request them even by accident.
  • No crash reports or diagnostics sent to us.
  • Nothing is sold or shared with anyone for marketing.

Who else is involved

  • Clerk — your phone number, to sign you in. Only if you turn on cloud backup.
  • Supabase — your synced records. Only if you turn on cloud backup.
  • Sarvam AI — questions the phone cannot answer, with names replaced by tokens; and voice recordings as they were made. Only if you connect a model key.

If you use none of the three, no third party receives anything, because the app makes no network requests at all.

Permissions, and why

  • Contacts — to import customers instead of typing them. Optional.
  • Microphone — to ask questions out loud. Optional.
  • Notifications — to show the reminders you set yourself.
  • Fingerprint or face unlock — to lock the app, if you switch that on. The check is done by your phone; Counter never sees your fingerprint or face.

Deleting everything

Cloud backup and account deletion are covered in detail on deleting your account. To erase the phone's own copy, use Settings → Start over. Both are immediate and neither can be undone.

Children

Counter is a tool for running a business and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect anything from anyone under 13.

Changes

If what the app sends ever changes, this page changes with it before the release ships, and the date below is updated. A privacy page that lags the app is worse than none.
